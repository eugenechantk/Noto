
# VERIFY: Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B (Qwen/Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B) — proposed as unified replacement for on-device iOS notes app

Verdict: NOT viable as a bundled unified replacement, but the report's reasoning needs two corrections that change the engineering picture. The binding constraint is confirmed and fatal for a ~300 MB bundle budget: 2.13B params means ~1.06 GB at int4 (~3.5x over budget) with no sub-1B family variant to fall back on (claims 2 and 6 both confirmed, and claim 6's falsification — the one that would most change the decision — did not occur). However, the report errs in two places: (1) its MMTEB retrieval subscore of 78.50 is actually the pair-classification column; real retrieval is 67.12 — still above the granite 60.3 bar but with a much thinner margin on a non-comparable task mix, so the 'text quality clears the bar' argument is weaker than stated; (2) the 'Core ML/ANE conversion is impractical' argument is refuted — mlboydaisuke's Core ML port of the identical Qwen3-VL-2B base (fixed 448x448 vision, INT8, runs on iPhone A18 ANE at 264 MB runtime memory in the stateful variant) proves fixed-shape conversion is practical, and 4-bit MLX builds of the embedding model itself already exist. The single biggest risk is therefore not convertibility but the ~1 GB weight footprint: it cannot ship in the bundle, forcing a post-install download plus ~1 GB+ of storage/memory pressure on iPhone-class devices for a notes app. If the team can accept downloaded-on-demand weights (as many on-device AI apps now do), the candidate becomes genuinely attractive — clean Apache-2.0 (verified down to the card text, unlike GME's hidden attribution clause), #1 in its size class and #4 overall on live MMEB-V2 (73.25), MRL 64-2048 for index compactness, and 32k context. If the 300 MB bundle budget is immovable, this candidate is out, and nothing in this family rescues it as of June 2026.

# VERIFY: FG-CLIP2-Base (Qihoo 360) — qihoo360/fg-clip2-base, proposed as image-leg for on-device iOS notes app

VIABLE — the candidate survives adversarial verification largely intact. The license story is clean (Apache-2.0 tag on HF, Apache-2.0 GitHub LICENSE, SigLIP2 init is Apache-2.0, and Google itself redistributes the Gemma 256k tokenizer ungated under Apache-2.0 in SigLIP2). The headline reason to pick it — Chinese retrieval at base size — is fully confirmed and the margins over both SigLIP2-B and Chinese-CLIP-B are large (COCO-CN T→I 62.9 vs 46.2/54.9). Two material corrections: (1) the image tower is ~93 MB int8 (92.93M params incl. the required attention-pool head), not ~87 MB, and the realistic quantized total is ~285+ MB rather than 280–285; (2) English 'costs nothing' is only true at matched 256px resolution and by razor-thin margins (Flickr +0.2, IN-1k +0.4) — FG-CLIP2's own comparison table shows it losing COCO T→I and IN-1k to the higher-res SigLIP2-B config. For a 256px on-device image-leg these are acceptable. The single biggest risk: the DIY conversion burden on the TEXT/query side — the text tower is not vanilla SigLIP2 (custom dual 196-length position-embedding tables with mask mixing, longtext_head, do_lower_case GemmaTokenizer semantics, all behind 1,400 lines of trust_remote_code with no official Core ML/ONNX artifacts and only a 15-download community proof point for the image side). If the Swift/Core ML text encoder mis-replicates any of these details, query and image embeddings silently land in misaligned spaces and retrieval quality degrades without an obvious error — budget for a numerical-parity test harness (cosine agreement vs the PyTorch reference) before committing.

# VERIFY: UForm3 image-text multilingual base (unum-cloud/uform3-image-text-multilingual-base) — proposed as fallback on-device image-text model for Noto

Still viable as a fallback — this candidate survives adversarial verification unusually well. Five of six claims check out against primary sources, including the exact byte sizes of the CoreML artifacts, the exact config.json limits, and the exact benchmark numbers on the model card; the only material refutation is freshness. For the fallback role specifically, everything that matters is true: Apache-2.0 on both HF and GitHub, ready-made ANE-quantized CoreML pair (~198 MB) plus tokenizer.json in-repo, a first-party Swift SDK targeting iOS 16+, sub-millisecond ANE text encoding, and genuinely multilingual (21 languages incl. Chinese) retrieval that beats OpenCLIP outside English. The single biggest risk: project dormancy compounded by a fragile dependency chain — no code commits since 2025-10-30, the Swift SDK untouched since June 2025 and pinned to a fixed 2024 revision of a personal swift-transformers fork (ashvardanian/swift-transformers), and unanswered quality issues. If an iOS/CoreML/Swift-toolchain update or an HF Hub API change breaks the SDK or fork, no upstream fix is coming; adopting UForm means budgeting to vendor and maintain ~1 file of Swift (Encoders.swift) and the fork pin yourself. Secondary risk worth carrying into the gating eval: the card's MS-COCO R@1 of 40.1 is flattered by admitted train-split contamination, so the planned EN+ZH bar on a Noto-like photo eval is mandatory, not optional — true zero-shot quality is lower than the headline numbers suggest. Practical mitigations: pin the HF revision (sha cd96b4d4) and bundle the .mlpackage artifacts in-app rather than relying on runtime Hub downloads, and remember the 50-token text limit means queries, not note bodies, are the only viable text input.

# VERIFY: SmolVLM2-500M (HuggingFaceTB/SmolVLM2-500M-Video-Instruct) — captioner for caption/OCR→granite baseline, fallback for on-device iOS notes app

Viable, but in a narrower role than the report implies, and one load-bearing claim is refuted. What survives verification: the license story is clean (Apache-2.0 vs Apple's genuinely research-only apple-amlr), the iPhone deployment proof exists (HuggingSnap on the App Store running the 500M via MLX), the CIVIL evidence is real and correctly caveated, and Apple's iOS 27 image-input announcement is confirmed — which means by fall 2026 this model's role really is stopgap plus pre-iPhone-15-Pro devices only. What fails: the size budget. No published image-capable int4 build of the 500M fits ≤300 MB — real ones are ~356 MB (OpenVINO) to ~412 MB (GGUF Q4 + vision mmproj), and the shipped iPhone demo runs ~1 GB MLX weights. The ~250–300 MB figure counts only the language-model half. The honest options are: (a) custom-quantize the vision tower to int4 (unproven, compounds the already-flagged unproven Core ML conversion), (b) drop to the 256M variant (~229 MB published, with unknown caption-quality loss), or (c) raise the budget to ~400 MB. Single biggest risk: the deployment footprint/runtime gap — the model has never been shipped in an image-capable form under 300 MB on any runtime, so the entire 'fits the budget' premise rests on a custom quantization-plus-conversion project that nobody has done, for a model whose strategic window (pre-iOS-27, older devices) is closing within months. Secondary risk worth a cheap pilot before any engineering spend: the ZH-query→EN-caption retrieval mechanism (claim 4) is the architecture's linchpin and has zero published evidence for this stack.
